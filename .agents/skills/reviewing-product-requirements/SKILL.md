# Review Product Requirements

Produce the smallest requirement draft that supports the requested decision.

1. Identify the actor, actual situation, desired observable progress, and
   requested solution from the supplied material. Separate user requirements
   from inferred intent, existing behavior, and unknowns. Do not invent personas,
   business targets, priorities, or policies.
2. Check whether the proposed means can achieve the stated result. Compare real
   alternatives only where a material decision exists; use the strongest case
   for each and identify evidence that would change the recommendation.
3. Resolve unknowns through authoritative product or repository material first.
   Ask one question when its answer changes scope or expected behavior and
   cannot be safely inferred. Continue independent drafting.
4. Describe in-scope behavior, non-goals, important state transitions, role or
   permission differences, failure and recovery expectations, and dependencies
   only as relevant. Trace each core outcome to an observable acceptance criterion.
5. Mark unresolved policy and scope choices as open, with their effect on delivery.
   A reasonable proposal is still a proposal until the decision is resolved.

Deliver concise requirements with sources, acceptance examples, and material
open decisions. Stop after the requested artifact. Do not convert a PRD request
into implementation, prescribe unnecessary UI flows, or claim stakeholder
agreement that has not occurred.
